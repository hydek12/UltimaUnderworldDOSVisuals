# Visual assessment and patch design

## Verified from the local copies

- DOS input is the inner `ultimaunderworld/uu` installation from `ultimaunderworld_dos_win.7z`. The 384-byte `CRIT/ASSOC.ANM` maps actor types to source animation and auxiliary palette. The 35×45 green goblin frame from type 70 uses hotspot (16, 43). Troll types 96, 111 and 112 share the same 32×52 front-facing frame and hotspot (15, 51), with auxiliary palettes 0, 2 and 1 respectively.
- The DOS files include 320×200 screen backgrounds, a 6-bit RGB palette bank, separate shade and transfer data, 64×64 wall textures, 16×16 and 32×32 floor texture banks, object and weapon sprites, conversation portraits, and separate cutscene resources. The measured tile populations are 210 entries in `W64.TR` and 52 entries each in `F16.TR` and `F32.TR`.
- Batocera input is `roms/psx/Ultima Underworld - The Stygian Abyss (English v2.0).chd`. Raw data Track 1 SHA-1 is `325585c11548538e323e8896c3fb8b1cc5d65521`. The disc also has three CD audio tracks. The 11,632,640-byte `DATA/AVATAR` archive begins at LBA 397 and contains 230 sector-aligned top-level entries. Its SHA-256 is `26493e897af94fd29177fcd26165e35a5c3447accb0a88acbb5fcc7f5e78fbe1`. It contains English text. The exact translation build is inferred from the filename. Asset roles are still unassigned. [English translation source](https://github.com/gertius1/UU_PSX_ENG)
- The assessment combines local DOSBox and DuckStation captures, DOS sprite and color analysis, and published footage. The in-game patch now covers all 13 Level 1 green goblins in idle and walking states. The remaining scene assets and UI still need a per-asset comparison. [Translation author’s PSX video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XuXlTk50Amk) · [Side-by-side observations and captures](https://www.wiki.ultimacodex.com/wiki/Technical_Differences_in_Ultima_Underworld_on_the_PSX)

The NPC audit walked all nine DOS tile maps and found 595 placed creature records across 63 types. The clean Japanese Rev 1 object archive has 657 creature records, including 550 initially linked by the native loader. Of the 595 DOS placements, 548 match a single exact PSX record, 45 match by type, conversation ID and spawn tile, one relocated dwarf matches by conversation ID, and one fire elemental has two exact unlinked candidates. All 550 initially linked PSX records have a single DOS candidate. All 63 placed DOS types have eight directional idle slots. Types 125 and 126 have no directional walk slots. The remaining 61 types have eight.

The four types sharing green goblin artwork use different auxiliary palettes. The disc covers all 13 initially linked Level 1 records. There are 19 more initially linked type 78 records on Levels 2, 5 and 7.

The DOS renderer selects a VGA remap row using the three-dimensional distance to a creature’s anchor and the active light level. The Xcode exporter calculates the row from `SHADES.DAT` and applies `LIGHT.DAT` or `MONO.DAT` while keeping the original alpha mask. The separate lighting preview uses the same calculation on the PSX and starts at DOS light level 0. The original DOS and the preview choose shade row 10 at the matched unlit Lanugo pose. Native PSX torches, spells, and visibility still need to drive the light-level selector. [Reconstructed DOS drawing routine](https://github.com/abedegno/underworld-exhumed/blob/54ffea7c08d562176a8edf7398a6ff3fd372a9d2/uw1/src/3d/GAMESORT.C#L330)

## Differences and target behavior

| Area | Observed release difference | DOS-fidelity target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In-world NPCs and enemies | DOS uses directional indexed sprites. PSX uses polygonal actors with its own silhouettes and motion. Published captures document swapped great/feral troll colors, altered ghost opacity and color, and a conspicuous purple shadow beast. | Render local DOS frames using a camera-facing world quad. Use original frame dimensions, hotspot, actor-relative facing, animation slots, palettes, and any confirmed transfer effects. |
| Motion and effects | Directional DOS frames are discrete. PSX has a different set of idle, attack and death motions. Fire and liquid appearance also differ. | Drive sprite state from PSX actor state. Calibrate animation cadence and directional boundaries against DOS. Retain gameplay event timing. |
| Viewport and camera | DOS has a framed 320×200 VGA display with a smaller, wider-angle world view. PSX uses a nearly full-screen world view with different geometry framing. | Match normalized DOS camera projection, viewport bounds and 4:3 presentation. Recompose the surrounding HUD and compare equivalent scene geometry before adjusting actor scale. |
| Lighting and color | VGA palette shading and transfer effects differ from PSX color and shading. DOS fire and lava remain bright and use palette animation. PSX scrolling flows and darkened emissives look different. | Reproduce DOS palette ramps, emissive classes, liquid cycles and ghost translucency. Convert to bounded PSX palette or GPU operations and compare dark, torch-lit and bright scenes. |
| Dungeon materials and mapping | Floors and ceilings were redrawn. Walls, doors, pillars, bridges, rocks, signs and decals differ in detail, size or texture. Inclined, diagonal and moving surfaces differ in texture clipping, stretch or pegging. | Map each PSX material ID to the matching DOS tile and test tile repetition, slope UVs, diagonal decals, raised platforms and moving textures. Fix projection or UV math when assets alone do not reproduce the same image. |
| Object sprites and geometry | PSX item art often drops DOS outlines. Fountains, portcullises and some furniture differ in silhouette, animation or visibility. Depth ordering can show geometry through other surfaces. | Restore original object appearance with DOS images where applicable. Match opaque and transparent pixels, anchor heights and occlusion. Investigate absent furniture and changed geometry separately. |
| Avatar, equipment and portraits | PSX restyles Avatar and NPC portraits and uses more unique conversation faces. Armor condition images are consolidated. | Map original DOS character creation, paper doll, armor damage stages and shared NPC portraits by identity and condition. |
| Weapons, spell art and UI | The console port rearranges inventory, spell, conversation and examination interfaces for its controls. Its icon art and message presentation differ. | Compare weapon idle and attack overlays plus spell effects, inventory, HUD, dialogue, fonts, cursor, icons, automap and status screens. Restore art and framing while allowing PSX inputs and translated text to fit. |
| Layout and visibility | Published captures report omitted thin walls, different doors and a raised fishing dock. These change the view even with identical materials. | Catalog matched scenes and check whether each change is presentation or level behavior. Keep map data changes separate and validate traversal and scripted interactions. |
| Intro, story and endings | DOS uses separate cutscene resources. PSX includes its own streamed movie files, including introductory, death and ending scenes. | Compare events by story beat and account for English subtitles and PSX playback constraints. Treat cutscenes as an optional milestone after dungeon presentation. |

## Remaining implementation and acceptance

1. Extend the gameplay-driven DOS lighting beyond the verified Level 1 bank. Compare native lantern behavior and DOS shade levels before giving additional room banks the DOS palettes.
2. Extend the four-palette path to the 19 initially linked green goblins on Levels 2, 5 and 7. The Level 2 texture loader uses VRAM x512–639, y384–511 for a native texture. Build a new texture layout or cache with room for the native texture, then check the dungeon, weapons and menus before enabling further floors.
3. Match combat actions to DOS slots, including the DOS front-angle restrictions on many attack frames. Keep hit and death timings, selection bounds and native fallback for unavailable art. Verify foot placement, occlusion and overlapping sprites.
4. Use the nine-floor crosswalk for the remaining creatures. Compare a named human in conversation, three troll variants, a ghost, and a shadow beast before moving to HUD, portraits, dungeon materials, objects, and map presentation. Record paired captures at comparable lighting and camera orientation.
5. Rebuild a scratch disc image using the hash-checked clean Japanese Rev 1 source. Preserve Mode 2 sector integrity and the three CD audio tracks. Boot each candidate in DuckStation, then test original-hardware and Batocera behavior. Check English dialogue, controller behavior, combat, music, saves, and level transitions.

## Future Avatar presentation

Identify which PlayStation scenes draw an Avatar mesh, including any character preview, and locate the corresponding asset and animation data. The DOS inventory renderer uses 2D `BODIES` art for the paper doll and `ARMOR_F` or `ARMOR_M` art for equipped armor. Check character creation, portraits, and first-person weapon art separately before deciding which surfaces to replace.

A future recipe can restore the matching DOS art directly on 2D surfaces. If an Avatar mesh is confirmed and no equivalent DOS mesh is found, use the DOS artwork as reference for the existing mesh's textures and silhouette. Prototype one scene and compare appearance, equipment layers, and animation before adding it as an opt-in recipe.
